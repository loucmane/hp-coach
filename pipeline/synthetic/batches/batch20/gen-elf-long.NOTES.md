## 1. Family / genre choice

`society_commentary`, written as reported commentary rather than as an essay, BrE
throughout.

Two reasons for the genre, both structural rather than aesthetic. First, the bank:
every ELF long since batch10 except the two batch14 reportage units and the two
history essays has been `science_journalism`, and the last four in a row
(b16-001 smoke taint, b17-001 contrails, b18-001 street-tree roots, b19-001 grain
dryers) are all science journalism built on a measured phenomenon with an expert
who has been through the files. A reported commentary on a **contractual** dispute
puts the evidence in a different place: the argument is about what a document
counts, not about what a mechanism does.

Second, the subject demands it. The assigned thesis — the metric measures the wrong
thing — only becomes concrete if the metric is a real clause with a threshold, a
tolerance, a payment and an author. All four exist here, and the author of the
clause is one of the three voices.

Register: journalism, mid-sentence-length, high variance (4 to 52 words, sd 12.4).
Numeric register is mixed on purpose (law 15): years in digits (2005, 2011, 2013,
2016, 2023), everything else spelled out (eighteen, twenty-seven, ninety-two per
cent, thirty-nine, fifty-four, seventy).

**Glossary:** none. One specialist term is used, *linkspan*, and it is glossed
inline in the sentence that introduces it (“the hinged ramp the vehicles drive
over”), so a glossary line would define nothing the passage has not already
defined. Law 15 asks that the glossary sometimes be dropped; this is one of those.

## 2. The thesis, and why it is specific rather than generic

This unit takes the batch's one permitted **“the metric measures the wrong thing”**
slot (law 12 caps it at ~1 per batch). Generic versions of this thesis say *the
number is the wrong number* and stop. Three things keep this one specific:

1. **The metric is measured against a document the measured party writes.**
   Punctuality here is not lateness against the world; it is lateness against the
   operator's own printed book. The operator sets the target it is then scored on.
2. **The mechanism from metric to harm is named and quantified.** Padding
   lengthens the booked crossing → the booked crossing sets the round-trip cycle →
   the cycle sets how many departures fit in a day → a passenger waits a *headway*,
   not a crossing. So minutes added to the book are paid for, several times over,
   by everybody who is not on the sailing that disappears. That chain, not the
   slogan, is what the questions turn on.
3. **The sharpest version of the thesis is delivered by the clause's own author,
   is about something else entirely, and nobody is arguing about it.** Bexwarden
   defends the clause on its consultation mandate and then volunteers that a
   cancelled sailing is not late — it leaves the count altogether. Her example is
   the quarter that lost forty-one sailings and scored the highest reliability
   figure in the crossing's history. The padding argument is loud; this one is not
   being had at all, and the tender draft carries the clause word for word.

The thesis is also deliberately **not vindicated**. Cattermay's measurement is
accepted by everyone; her *causal* claim is confounded by the withdrawal of the
relief boat in the middle of her own series, and she says so herself. That is the
un-tidiness law 9 asks for: the piece cannot resolve, and does not pretend to.

## 5. Trap design — named operation on a named target

Every distractor is a named transformation of something the passage actually says.

**Q1 — ELF-TYPE-001, paragraph 1, the quality clause. Key C.**
- **A** *quantifier_upgrade, two axes at once*: the threshold (ninety-two per cent →
  “every sailing”) and the tolerance (ten minutes → “exactly on schedule”). Tempting
  because it is the shape a reader expects a punctuality rule to have.
- **B** *wrong_location splice*: both dates are in the text (clause 2011, boat 2016)
  and the text never joins them; the five-year gap refutes the join.
- **D** *outside_knowledge*, and the strongest trap in the unit: real performance
  regimes usually **do** dock an operator for cancellations. Paragraph 4 says the
  opposite. Domain knowledge actively hurts here, which is the point.

**Q2 — ELF-TYPE-001, paragraph 3, Cottam on the 2005 book. Key A.**
- **B** *quantifier_upgrade*: a median is not every sailing.
- **C** *hedged-but-unsupported*: “broadly realistic except in the worst of the
  winter weather” is the careful-sounding answer and is exactly the reading Cottam
  rejects.
- **D** *wrong_location*: a skimmer dates measurement to the 2011 clause; paragraph 1
  publishes a reliability figure for 2005, six years earlier.

**Q3 — ELF-TYPE-002, paragraph 3 read against paragraph 5. Key D.**
The key is a one-inch inference from arithmetic the text supplies and never adds up:
two boats and the old book gave a departure every 25 minutes; the 2013 book took the
interval to 32 while there were still two boats; one boat gives 70. So the book is
worth about seven minutes of interval and the lost boat about thirty-eight.
- **A** *too_literal with an actor-level distortion*: the text says the certificate
  ran out and the company did not replace her, and says nothing about a sale — law 11
  applied to a TYPE-002 literal trap, so it is retrieval nudged off the facts rather
  than a fully-true option.
- **B** *two-step leap + reversed causality*: longer gaps are a consequence of running
  one boat, not a motive for withdrawing the second.
- **C** *overgeneralisation*: Cottam's position hardened into an absolute
  (“Nothing … can be blamed on the timetable”), which paragraph 5 refutes in his own
  case.

**Q4 — ELF-TYPE-005, whole text, resolved in paragraph 5. Key B.**
- **A** *role_or_attribution_swap*, this family's signature: the operator's position
  handed to the writer. Paragraph 5 refuses it by naming a soft place in Cottam's
  own account.
- **C** *polarity overshoot*: “the evidence cannot support at all” is dismissal,
  where the text says the explanation *fits* but has not been tested.
- **D** *neutral-reporter misread*, available only if paragraph 5 is skipped.

**Q5 — ELF-TYPE-004, main idea, edge position 5. Key D.**
- **A** *scope_error*: the linkspan sub-point promoted to subject.
- **B** *too-broad, title-plausible theme*: the text is about one crossing under one
  contract and never generalises.
- **C** *cautious-sounding scope error*, placed deliberately: it borrows the genuine
  limitation the writer states and still mistakes a difficulty inside the argument
  for the subject of it.

## 6. Self-blind-solve (law 4)

Solved from the passage alone, arguing **for** each non-key option first and killing
it on a cited span. Result: exactly one defensible answer per question, 5/5.

**Q1 — key C.**
- *For A*: performance clauses are usually written as “on time or not”, so “every
  sailing … exactly on schedule” is the natural reading. **Killed on** “at least
  ninety-two per cent of its sailings berth within ten minutes of the booked minute”
  — both the threshold and the ten-minute window are explicit.
- *For B*: the clause and the withdrawal are the two contractual events in the piece,
  so a reader may join them. **Killed on** “Since 2011” (para 1) against “in 2016 the
  relief boat's passenger certificate ran out” (para 3).
- *For D*: this is what a transport economist would expect, and “reduced, though not
  withdrawn” sounds like the measured answer. **Killed on** “A sailing that does not
  run is not late; it leaves the count altogether” — a cancellation touches neither
  the numerator nor the payment. This is the closest call in the unit and it is
  closest for readers who know the domain, which is the intended direction.
- *C* is the only option that reproduces both the proportion and the tolerance.

**Q2 — key A.**
- *For B*: “Every sailing … arrived later” follows if you read a median as a rule.
  **Killed on** “The median crossing that year took twenty-four minutes” — a median
  is a central value, not a universal.
- *For C*: it is the cautious answer, and the piece does mention weather elsewhere.
  **Killed on** absence: Cottam's whole first move is that the old book was
  unachievable in the ordinary case, not the exceptional one, and no weather
  qualification is attached to it anywhere.
- *For D*: measurement feels like it starts with the 2011 clause. **Killed on** “The
  published reliability figure stood at seventy-one per cent in 2005.”
- *A* is the only option that states what the twenty-four-against-eighteen comparison
  shows.

**Q3 — key D.**
- *For A*: it is the most literally accurate-sounding sentence available. **Killed
  on** “the company did not replace her” — the text says nothing about a sale, and A
  asserts one; it is also retrieval, not implication, so it fails the stem.
- *For B*: intervals did lengthen after the withdrawal, so a motive reading is
  available. **Killed on** direction: Cottam's own line (“One boat gives you one
  every seventy”) presents the interval as the consequence of running one boat, and
  no motive is stated by anyone.
- *For C*: it is Cottam's position, stated forcefully. **Killed on** paragraph 5 —
  “the interval between departures went from twenty-five minutes to thirty-two …
  Some of the frequency was gone before the boat was.” The absolute is exactly what
  the writer withdraws.
- *D* survives the arithmetic and nothing else does.

**Q4 — key B.**
- *For A*: Cottam gets the longest run of concrete detail, and paragraph 5 opens by
  conceding to him. **Killed on** “Cottam's account has a soft place in the same
  decade” and the correction that follows it.
- *For C*: the writer does say her chain is unproven. **Killed on** “the timetable is
  an explanation that **fits** without having been tested” — fitting is not failing.
- *For D*: the piece is reported and quotes everyone. **Killed on** paragraph 5,
  which judges both principals and names what none of them is arguing about.
- *B* is the only option that separates the measurement from the cause, which is the
  distinction paragraph 5 is built on.

**Q5 — key D.**
- *For A*: the linkspan is the most technical thing in the piece. **Killed on**
  proportion — it occupies two sentences of paragraph 3 and one clause of paragraph 5.
- *For B*: “run down” fits the mood. **Killed on** absence: no other route, operator
  or region is mentioned; the piece never leaves this crossing.
- *For C*: the writer really does say the series cannot be separated, so this is the
  option a careful reader is drawn to. **Killed on** scope — the difficulty is one
  move inside the argument, and it accounts for neither the rising figure nor
  Bexwarden's cancellation point.
- *D* is the only option that holds all three parts the text actually runs on.

**Adversarial stems-only floor (options and stems, passage unopened).** Q1 and Q2
are guessable at about 1-in-3 from option-set shape; Q3 and Q4 at 1-in-4; Q5 at about
1-in-2, because main-idea items always leak some structure. Measured **as a sheet**
the floor is no better than 1-in-2 on any single item, and no cross-question chain
raises it — see §8.

## 11. Laws I found myself in tension with, and how I resolved them

**(a) RULE 17 (an ordinary, unremarkable name) versus law 16 as the brief states it
(“an exact real-place or real-bearer match = reject the name”).**
Read strictly, the second makes the first impossible: every ordinary English name has
bearers, and that is *what makes it ordinary*. Resolved by applying to the ordinary
figure the test that actually fits an ordinary name — **no notable bearer**
(en.wikipedia exact phrase `"Trevor Cottam"` → 0) and **no bearer in the ferry,
transport-statistics or maritime-regulation domain** — and by saying plainly in the
search log that Cottam and Trevor are ordinary real names with many private bearers.
The domain leg is not decorative: an earlier candidate for the same slot, *Trevor
Dodd*, cleared the notability test (en.wiki 0 for the pair) and was dropped anyway,
because one real bearer is a UK freight forwarder. The strict-homonym reading is kept
in force for the three *coined* names, where it belongs.

**(b) The two-index requirement of the law-16 recipe versus the tools available.**
Not resolvable this session — see §10. Recorded as a genuine weakening rather than
papered over, and used as the stated reason for the V-FINAL flag.

**(c) Law 12's “retire the aphoristic close” versus the temptation of the strongest
material.** Bexwarden's cancellation point is the sharpest line in the piece and
wants to be the last line. Putting it there would have produced exactly the
aphoristic two-sentence close law 12 retires, and would have resolved a passage that
should not resolve. It sits in paragraph 4 instead, and the piece ends on the tender
draft — flat, factual, and no verdict attached. The previous ELF long ended on a
rhetorical question, so that exit was closed too.
