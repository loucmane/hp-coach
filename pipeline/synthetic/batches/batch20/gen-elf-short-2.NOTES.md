## 1. Move sequence — diffed against every shipped TYPE-002 short

The five shipped TYPE-002 history-essay shorts (`elf-b15-004` Quarnley Bar,

3. two dated figures set side by side;
4. one clause that blocks the obvious reading;
5. a coda that either dates the practice or records an archival silence;
6. byline.

**What this unit does instead.**

1. **Opens on a person in close focus, not on an institution.** "John Ward was

2. **Carries no tariff.** There is no table of charges organising the piece.
   Money appears twice, as two particular premiums and as a clerk's fee, and

3. **Widens from the one case to the series, then narrows to the constraint.**

4. **Closes on the clerk's own fee**, set twice against one name. Not a
   date-the-practice coda (saturated, and barred for the LÄS long lane by

   (`elf-b15-004` "does not mention the summer", `elf-b19-004` "remarked only

   a piece of bureaucratic residue.
5. **Past tense throughout.** The batch18 additions list *document-reading
   present tense* as saturated, and `elf-b18-004` ("The plan of 1848 prices
   every pew in ink") is the unit that saturated it. Nothing here is read in

## 2. Trap design

| opt | claim | trap | how the passage defeats it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A (key)** | bound for years beyond the first term's scheduled end | — | seven-year invariant + "not continuations… ran its seven years from the day of sealing" + "three to six years after the first"; 3–6 < 7 |
| B | lost their first places when the masters gave up their trades | master-failure misread — causal glue plus the reader's default social-history story for a broken indenture | "Each of the first masters went on enrolling boys of his own for years afterwards" — every one of those men still in trade, still taking apprentices |
| C | placed a second time by families living in the town | local-connections misread — an origins claim that inverts the record, and smuggles in an agent the passage never names | two of five fathers in the town; one farmed nine miles off; one combed wool in the next county; one the clerk left unplaced |
| D | went into their second trades at a smaller premium | discount-for-damaged-goods misread — the plausible economics | Ward's £11 → £14; and "In three of the five the second premium was the larger sum" |

Each distractor sits on **its own variable** — masters, origins, money —
and none of them is on the key's variable, which is *the length of the
obligation*. That is deliberate and it is the structural answer to the
escalation: with four variables and four options there is no pair for a
test-wise reader to isolate.

**What the key deliberately does not claim.** Not that the five finished, not
that they were made free, not what became of them. Fifty-seven bindings carry
no note of service and the five may be among them. An earlier draft's key
("they came out of their apprenticeships later…") assumed completion and was
therefore *unsupported*; it was rewritten to speak only of the obligation the
second indenture created, which the sealing dates settle on their own.

## 3. OPTION-ONLY SORTABILITY TEST (the batch's live escalation)

Run as instructed: passage covered, four option texts alone.

```
A  They were bound for years beyond the day their first term would have ended.
B  The five lost their first places when the masters who held them gave up their trades.
C  They were placed a second time by families living in the town.
D  The five went into their second trades at a smaller premium than the first had cost.
```

**What a candidate CAN infer from these four texts alone**

- the domain: an apprenticeship register; five boys were bound twice;
- that a first binding and a second binding are both in play;
- four candidate facts about those five: how long they stayed bound (A), what
  happened to their first places (B), who arranged the second (C), what the
  second cost (D);
- that the passage contains masters, families, a town, terms and premiums.

**What a candidate CANNOT infer — axis by axis**

1. **Contradictory pair: none.** No two options are jointly exhaustive or
   mutually exclusive. A boy could simultaneously have lost his place to a
   master who quit (B), been placed again by his town family (C), paid less
   (D) *and* still be bound past his first term's end (A). Because no pair
   partitions a variable, the heuristic "the key is one of the two that
   disagree" has nothing to operate on. This is the specific failure of
   `elf-b19-004`, where B and D were near-contradictories on one axis while A
   and C sat off it.
2. **Odd one out by shape: none.** All four are past-tense declaratives whose
   subject denotes the same five people. Two open *They* (A, C) and two open
   *The five* (B, D). Two are passive (A, C) and two active (B, D). All four
   end on a subordinated tail (a *beyond*-phrase, a *when*-clause, a *by*-
   phrase, a *than*-clause).
3. **Hedging axis: degenerate.** No option contains a softener
   (*may / might / probably / chiefly / mainly / largely / some / several*)
   and no option contains an absolutizer from `mech.py`'s `_ABSOLUTIZERS`
   (*always / never / every / all / only / none / entirely / impossible /
   proves / guarantees / certainly*, plus the efterhandstillägg additions
   *nothing / nobody / no one*). "Pick the qualified one" and "strip the
   absolutes and take what is left" both return the empty set. Removing the
   axis is stronger than inverting it: `elf-b19-004` put the sheet's single
   hedge on a distractor, which still leaves a reader something to notice.
4. **Motive-versus-evidence axis: none.** Not one option asserts an intention,
   a motive or a state of mind. All four assert events. There is no
   "unobservable motive" option to discard, which was one of the two shapes
   that let `elf-b19-004` be halved.
5. **Scope: no singleton.** All four are scoped to exactly the same
   population — the five. None generalises to the register, the borough, the
   trade or the period; none narrows to a single boy. There is no
   "single-institution" option, the other of `elf-b19-004`'s two shapes.
6. **Singular vs plural: uniform.** All four subjects are plural and denote
   the five. Nothing to sort.
7. **Length: uninformative.** 14 / 16 / 12 / 16 words. The key is neither the
   longest nor the shortest; two distractors are longer and one is shorter, so
   "pick the longest" and "pick the shortest" both select a distractor.

**Finding, stated honestly.** I can get to **1-in-4 and no better** on the
option texts alone. The only two asymmetries I can find are that **C** is the
shortest by two words and is the only option carrying an explicit agent phrase
("by families"). Neither is a documented key heuristic, and — this is the part
that matters — **both point at a distractor**, so a candidate who did exploit
them would be pulled away from the key rather than toward it. I could not
construct a rule over the four wordings that beats chance.

**The draft that failed this test, and was rebuilt.** The first version of this
sheet had key "the years already served were lost" against a distractor "the
years already served were reckoned against the second term" — a
near-contradictory pair on one variable, which is exactly the `elf-b19-004`
shape and would have advertised where the key lived. It also had the key as the
sole *consequence-for-the-boy* claim with three *circumstance* claims beside
it, a second structural singleton. Both were engineered out before the file was
written: the term-crediting variable was removed from the sheet entirely rather
than balanced, and all four options were rewritten as consequence claims about
the same five people.

**Stem-plus-options floor.** The stem
("What is suggested about the five boys who were bound twice?") discloses only
the population the options already name, so the joint stems-and-options floor
is **1-in-4**, i.e. chance. The title, *Ward's Two Indentures*, names the
anecdote and not the pattern, and no option mentions Ward, so it does not move
the floor either.

## 4. Self-blind-solve — arguing FOR each distractor before killing it

Run from the passage alone, after a break from the drafting.

**B — the masters gave up their trades.** *For:* this is the reader's default
story and the passage supplies the hook for it. Fifty-seven bindings out of
211 carry no note of service; the passage never says why any indenture ended;
in this period the commonest lawful end to an apprenticeship short of its term
was the master's death or failure, at which point the boy was loose and had to
be placed again. If you have any social history at all, B is what you reach
for. *Killed by:* "Each of the first masters went on enrolling boys of his own
for years afterwards." That puts every one of those men still in his trade and
still taking apprentices *after* the break. Whatever ended the five first
bindings, it was not the master leaving the trade. Note that the passage does
not announce this refutation — it states a fact about the masters and lets the
reader do the work.

**C — town families placed them again.** *For:* two of the five fathers did
live in the town; a second binding plainly needed somebody to arrange and pay
for it; and the passage is careful to say that both entries name the father,
his trade and his parish, which invites the thought that family proximity is
what made a second start possible. It is also the most natural *mobility*
reading, and this is a mobility passage. *Killed by:* the count. Two of five is
not a description of the five. One father farmed nine miles off, one combed
wool in the next county, and one the clerk left unplaced — so a majority
of the five had no family in the town. It dies a second time on the agent: the
passage never says who arranged any second binding, and C asserts that it was
the family.

**D — the second trade cost less.** *For:* the sensible economics, and the
passage puts premiums in front of the reader twice, which reads like an
invitation. A master taking on a boy who had already broken one indenture might
reasonably ask less; and if a boy was stepping down into a humbler trade the
premium would fall with it. *Killed by:* two separate statements. Ward's own
premium rose, £11 at the saddler's to £14 at the whitesmith's; and "In three of
the five the second premium was the larger sum" refutes it for the group as
well as for the named case. D is right that the premium is worth looking at and
wrong about which way it moved.

**A — bound past the first term's end.** *Survives.* The chain is: the book
holds no term but seven years; the second entries were not continuations, each
running its seven years from its own sealing date; each fell three to six years
after the binding it followed; three to six is strictly less than seven. So a
second term sealed three to six years into a seven-year term must still be
running when the first would have expired, and must run three to six years past
it. The passage states each link and joins none of them. For Ward the concrete
form of it is 1751 + 7 = 1758 against 1755 + 7 = 1762.

**Verdict: exactly one defensible answer.** I could not construct a second, and
I could not reach A from the options alone.

## 8. Period detail an audit will want to check

- **Seven-year term.** The Statute of Artificers 1563 set a seven-year minimum
  and it held until the apprenticeship clauses were repealed in 1814, so a book
  covering 1746–1771 with "no term but seven years" is right. The passage makes
  the invariant explicit *because the key depends on it*.
- **Who was bound to whom.** The indenture named the apprentice, the master and
  the trade; the boy's father or friends were parties who covenanted and paid.
  A borough register recording the father's name, trade and parish is standard
  practice — and it is the father's abode that makes two entries joinable,
  which is why the passage says so.
- **Why a borough enrolled at all.** In a corporate borough the freedom came by
  servitude, so the corporation kept its own enrolment of indentures and
  charged a fee for it. Two shillings is an ordinary clerk's fee.
- **Premiums.** £11 and £14 sit inside the ordinary provincial range for craft
  trades in the 1750s — a few pounds for the humblest trades, tens of pounds
  for the better ones, hundreds only for merchants and the professions. The
  figures are deliberately unremarkable rather than striking.
- **Trades.** Saddler, **whitesmith** (a finisher and worker of tinned or
  bright iron, distinct from a blacksmith) and wool-comber are all real
  eighteenth-century occupations.
- **Non-completion.** 57 of 211, about 27 %, is a plausible shortfall for a
  provincial register; completion rates well under three-quarters are
  unremarkable.
- **Turning over.** Assignment of the unexpired term to a new master was the
  normal remedy when a master died or failed. The passage marks its **absence**
  explicitly ("The second entries were not continuations"), and that is what
  licenses the key: had they been assignments the arithmetic would not run.
- **Age.** A boy bound at about fourteen in 1751 and bound afresh in 1755 comes
  out at about twenty-five instead of twenty-one. That is the concrete human
  content of key A, and full fresh terms for re-bound apprentices are well
  attested.
- **What the record would NOT hold.** Why a boy left, where he went if he left
  the town, what a second master thought of him. The unit asserts none of
  those, and the key is written so that it does not need to.
