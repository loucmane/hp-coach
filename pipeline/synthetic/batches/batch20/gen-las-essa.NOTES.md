## 1. Genre, topic, and what the passage is doing

Assigned subject: hand-coloured picture postcards and what the colourist changed
between the negative and the printed card. The passage is a `sakprosa /
essa_kulturhistoria` short: an essayist has laid a small provincial publisher's
surviving glass plates beside the cards printed from them, and reports what the
comparison shows.

The essay's actual thesis is a **sorting thesis**, not a verdict: the differences
between the place and the card do not come from one place. Some are the
*process* (the plate could not hold a sky, so a sky had to be supplied), some are
the *counter* (nobody bought a card of a washing line and a muddy street), and
some are the *stock room* (the firm reprinted from an old plate for as long as a
view sold, so a demolished building can stand on a card postmarked eight years
later without anyone having painted it back). And the sorting **fails** at the
people: a man in a doorway on the plate is gone from the card, and the transfer
itself eats small grey things, so removal and loss are not separable.

Register markers: nominalisations (*fotograferingen, tryckningen, sorteringen,
överföringen*), `-s`-passives (*beställdes, lades, målades, gjordes*), one light
first person (*Jag har lagt plåt och kort bredvid varandra*), two glossed
specialist terms.

## 3. Trap architecture

### Q1 — *Vad framkom om himlen vid jämförelsen?* (key **B**)

Target: three linked sentences in ¶3 — *Himlen ändras på varje kort. På de flesta
plåtarna är den en tom vit yta… Nio plåtar har moln, och även på de korten ligger
samma jämna blå himmel över taken.* Hedged/directional/scoped: the direction is
plate → card, the scope is this firm, and the load is on *även*.

| opt | trap | why it tempts / why it fails |
|---|---|---|
| A | `scope_shift` | narrows the finding to the blank plates. The word *även* exists in the passage precisely to close this reading. |
| **B** | **key** | paraphrase of the target: the blue went on regardless of what the plate already carried. |
| C | invented variation + `surface_lexical_echo` | reads like a finding and carries the same surprise marker (*även*) as the key, so that marker cannot pick the answer out on its own. Text says *samma jämna blå himmel*. |
| D | `plausible_worldknowledge` (inverted) | a solver who knows orthochromatic plates blow out skies concludes the sky was left white. Flatly contradicted by *Himlen ändras på varje kort*. **World knowledge is punished here, not rewarded.** |

### Q2 — *Vad talar för att en poststämpel säger lite om när bilden togs?* (key **D**)

Two-fact inference: ¶1's magasin (card postmarked August 1919, building
demolished autumn 1911) plus ¶4's *Vidbring tryckte nya upplagor från samma plåt
så länge ett motiv gick att sälja*. The passage never joins them for the reader.

| opt | trap | why it tempts / why it fails |
|---|---|---|
| A | `plausible_worldknowledge` | bought-in older images would also make a picture older than its print — **logically apt, textually absent**; the text says the firm photographed on glass plate itself and never mentions buying in. |
| B | `reversed_causality` | contradicted (*samma anvisning följde med varje gång*), and a remade motif would make the card *more* time-specific, not less. |
| C | `true_but_unsupported` | long shelf time would also push the postmark forward — again logically apt, but nothing in the text is about how long cards sat in shops. |
| **D** | **key** | paraphrase of the one fact the passage actually supplies. |

A and C are deliberately built to be *logically capable* of supporting the stem's
proposition, so the stem alone cannot settle the item — see §4.

## 4. RULE 15 — the PAIR check, stated honestly

**Neither question's option field is a mechanism inventory over the essay's
hypothesis space, and the two option fields do not overlap.** The essay's
hypothesis space is the three-way taxonomy (process / counter / stock). Q1's
options are four competing factual claims about one concrete finding (the sky);
Q2's options are four candidate *supporting facts* for a dating claim. Neither
set enumerates the taxonomy, and neither is a stance question — there is no
"what does the author think" item in this unit at all. The failure mode that
produced RULE 15 (Q1 reprints the hypothesis space, Q2 asks which of those
positions the author holds) is structurally absent.

**Joint stems-only floor (stems + 8 option texts, no passage, no title, no
rationales): ≈ 30–35 %, i.e. a little above the 25 % chance floor and no better
than 1-in-3.** Working it through:

- *Q1 alone*: the four claims are equally shaped as findings. My first draft was
  worse — the key was the only option carrying the surprise marker *även*, and I
  estimated 45–55 % on rhetorical shape alone. I therefore rewrote distractor C
  so that it carries *även* too and reads equally like a reported finding. With
  that fix a blind reader is choosing between two surprise-shaped options and two
  flat ones: **≈ 35 %**.
- *Q2 alone*: the stem asserts its conclusion and asks for support, so an option
  that cannot support the conclusion is eliminable without the passage. B is such
  an option (a remade motif argues the other way), so a logical solver is down to
  three. My first draft had only D logically apt and I estimated 50–60 %; I
  therefore rebuilt A and C to be logically apt but textually absent. **≈ 33 %**.
- *The pair*: Q1 discloses nothing that helps Q2 and Q2 discloses nothing that
  helps Q1. There is no shared hypothesis space, no shared referent beyond the
  passage's unavoidable subject, and no direction of dependency between the
  items — reading Q1's options does not change the odds on Q2, or the reverse.
  The joint floor is therefore **the max of the two, ≈ 35 %, not their sum**.
  There is one weak generic heuristic available across the sheet — "this essay is
  about a colourist adding things" — and it is worth noting that it points at
  Q1's key but **away** from Q2's key, whose whole content is that this
  particular difference is *not* the colourist's doing. The cross-question
  heuristic is therefore actively harmful, which is the property RULE 15 wants.

## 7. Self-blind-solve (passage open, arguing actively for each distractor)

**Q1.** A: *only where the plate was blank* — I argued for it: most plates were
blank, so "coloured where it was blank" is true of most cards. It fails on
*även på de korten*, which explicitly covers the nine cloud plates; A's *bara*
is the falsifiable word. C: *different colour on identical views* — nothing in
the text supports variation, and *samma jämna blå himmel* denies it. D: *left
white in most cases* — the strongest pull, because *På de flesta plåtarna är den
en tom vit yta* is right there; but that sentence is about the **plate**, and the
paragraph opens *Himlen ändras på varje kort*. One defensible answer: **B**.

**Q2.** A: *bought-in older images* — genuinely tempting because it would explain
the gap, but the passage attributes the photography to the firm and never
mentions purchase, so it is unsupported invention. B: fails twice, contradicted
and pointing the wrong way. C: shelf time would move the postmark, not the
exposure, and in any case the passage says nothing about shops holding stock —
its stock claim is about *plates*, not cards. D is the only account the passage
supplies. One defensible answer: **D**.

Law 11 check: no distractor in either question is verbatim-true per the passage;
each carries a locatable flaw (a *bara*, a denied variation, a wrong-direction
claim, or a claim the passage never makes).
