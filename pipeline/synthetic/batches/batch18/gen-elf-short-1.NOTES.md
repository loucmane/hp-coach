## Fact base (see generator_meta.mechanism_verification_note for the log)

Wienerberger technical note "Dr John notes: Torching": lime + horsehair;
outdated late 1930s when bitumen underfelt spread; not recommended now because
capillary attraction where torching contacts battens introduces dampness and
eventual decay, against current practice and British Standards; purpose was
draughts/driving-snow ingress, now performed by underlay. Nibs hook over
battens; "unless the roof exposure is high, only every fourth course of tiles
is nailed" (designingbuildings.co.uk; SPAB; buildingconservation.com) — hence
the passage's hedged "often only every fourth course". Underlay sits over
rafters, under battens ("felt under the battens"). A lime-conservation
counter-view exists (breathability advocacy); the passage concedes torching
worked at its job and asserts only the mainstream decay account.
