## 1. Topic choice

Assigned three options; took **(c) grain-dryer economics and why harvest-time
fires cluster in specific dryer types**.

- **(a) concert-hall reverberation / movable banners** was rejected as
  domain-adjacent to the shipped `kontorsakustik-popularvetenskap-short`
  (room acoustics, absorptive treatment, users complaining about the result) —
  a graze on an excluded family in everything but language.
- **(b) bridge-bearing replacement under live traffic** was rejected because
  its whole hook is a **millimetre budget** measured on a schedule, which is
  the motif the batch19 addendum marks saturated ("wedge-gauge/measurement-book
  codas") and which batch18's `elf-b18-001` spent a paragraph on. Two
  consecutive ELF longs whose evidence is a tolerance survey would be an
  architectural clone in everything but subject.
- **(c)** carries the law-1 test properly. A dryer engineer *knows* the answer
  to "why do dryers catch fire" — fines in the ducts — and that knowledge is
  precisely what the passage confirms as the wrong reason. An expert answering
  Q2 from the field would pick A (the fines story) and be wrong; Q3, Q4 and Q5
  turn on invented objections, an invented clustering statistic and the
  writer's own split verdict, none of which exist outside this text. The
  domain also joins engineering to economics without either half being a
  textbook thesis.

**Nothing real is named.** No place, county, firm, cooperative, insurer,
product line, standard or publication appears — only four invented people and
one invented byline. Mixed-flow and cross-flow dryers, duct fines, moisture
deductions and custom drying are generic domain furniture, not identifiable
entities.

## 6. RULE 13 / law 10 — hedge balance

Pick-the-qualified-option selects the key in **2 of 5** (Q4's scoped "whatever
they own", Q5's two-part verdict) — at or below half.

Rule-10 breaks, i.e. questions where the key is the **confident, specific,
absolute-free claim** and a cautious-sounding option is wrong:

- **Q2** — key "came out about equally dusty inside" is flat and specific;
  distractor D "Too few units were sampled for the comparison to mean much"
  is the modest-sounding wrong answer. (The passage was edited for this: an
  earlier draft had Grendisham say "twenty-six machines is a sample", which
  gave D a real foothold and risked a second defensible key. Her objection is
  now purely about *when* you swab — September clean, February full — so D
  asserts a limitation nobody in the text states.)
- **Q3** — key "They mark when a fire was noticed, not when it began" is flat;
  the two hedge-shaped or absolute options (B "only", D "never … at all") are
  wrong.

Absolutizer distribution: Q1 none anywhere · Q2 one distractor (*all*) · Q3
two distractors (*only*; *never*, *all*) with distractor A clean · Q4 one
distractor (*every*) · Q5 none anywhere. **No question** has every distractor
absolutized against a measured key, so strip-the-absolutes is dead — M-FORM
passes structurally, not by luck.
