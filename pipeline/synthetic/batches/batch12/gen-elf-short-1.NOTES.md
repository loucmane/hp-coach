# gen-elf-short-1 — "Naming the Wall" (batch 12)

**Block format:** `short_text_1q` · **Genre:** society_commentary · **Spelling variety:** AmE (`color`, `harbor`, `toward`, `recognized`)
**Family:** ELF-TYPE-001 (direct detail retrieval) · **Key:** B

## Why this topic and this cut of it

Paint-shade names invite a whimsy piece. This one is written as trade
procedure: the naming happens *last*, after the pigments are fixed, by two
people with a spreadsheet, and it is governed by one working rule — a name must
not be checkable. The rule is then operationalized by a contrast the reader can
test (Harbor Fog vs Tomato), and the passage draws the consequence: names drift
toward unphotographed weather and places nobody has been. That is a
passage-specific claim, not a stateable outside fact, so a solver who knows the
paint industry gains nothing (law 1). Bellamy & Roe, Odile Brennan and both
shade names are invented; no real manufacturer or trademarked shade is used.

Deliberate residue (law 9): "forty shades", "two people with a spreadsheet" and
the closing list "brick, ash, sand" are never tested. The last sentence also
concedes an exception to the rule rather than tidying it away.

## Trap architecture

Stem: *What are we told about the rule that Bellamy & Roe apply when naming a
shade?* — the attested "What are we told about …" form. It names the setup (a
rule exists) without entailing its content, so the stem does not leak the key.

| opt | role | mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| A | wrong order of operations | customer testing *before* the pigments are settled — a plausible thing a paint firm would do, but the passage states the reverse sequence ("settled last, after the pigments are fixed") and describes no testing at all |
| **B** | **key** | paraphrase of the stated rule joined to its worked example: a handle "must not be checkable"; Harbor Fog cannot be found wrong, Tomato can, and the tomato-grower "will say so at the counter" |
| C | the reader's prior belief | that a good name describes the shade — exactly the position sentence 1 denies ("the names are not descriptions"); attractive because it is what most buyers assume |
| D | surface_word_match, wrong auditor | recycles the checkability idea but audits the name against an industry color standard; the passage's only judge is a customer, and no trade standard is mentioned |

No distractor is verbatim-true (law 11): each carries one identifiable flaw —
reversed sequence (A), direct contradiction of the opening claim (C), invented
auditor (D).

## Self-blind-solve

Argued all four from the passage alone. **A** is the strongest rival, because
"a customer … will say so at the counter" can be misread as feedback the firm
uses — but that is a customer catching a name out *after* it ships, and the
naming sequence is stated explicitly against A. **C** is refuted by the first
sentence. **D** has the right idea attached to the wrong party. **Single
defensible answer; no rewrite needed.**

Test-wise checks: all four options are confident, unhedged assertions and the
key is the *specific* one, so "pick the qualified answer" does not locate it —
this unit deliberately carries the confident key, against short-2's measured
one. The only absolutizer in the set ("only", D) sits on a distractor, so
M-FORM's strip-the-absolutes shortcut fails. Key is 16 words against 16 / 15 /
18 — joint-second, not the longest. All four options open with "That" and are
grammatically parallel.
