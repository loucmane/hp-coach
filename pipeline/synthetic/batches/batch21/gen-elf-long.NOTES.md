## 2. The mechanism (what the passage is actually for)

One structure, two species, **two different failure points**:

- **Dace never reach the entrance.** They run the margins in loose shoals; at Ferningate
  the right margin is a long slack behind a shingle bar, so a dace arrives on the far
  side from the entrance with thirty metres of broken water in between. The pass
  releases about 0.25 m3/s into a river bringing down 9 in April, so the plume is gone
  four metres out. Tags: 212 released, 174 read at the weir, only **23** on the entrance
  side, 9 in, 5 out.
- **Grayling reach the entrance easily and are stopped inside.** They hold in the
  current, so 54 of 61 come up the correct bank and 39 of them enter — and 12 come out.
  Deep body, dorsal fin like a sail, and a fish that does not leave the water, so every
  notch is a burst through the jet; the burst falls off with temperature (11 entered
  above ten degrees, 7 out; 28 entered colder, 5 out) and the pools are too short to
  hold resting water.
- **Trout are never tagged at all** — they run in November and December, and in three of
  the fifteen winters the river drowned the weir out. Deliberate residue, not decoration.

Arithmetic reconciles on the bytes: 11+28 = 39 and 7+5 = 12; nine cross-walls at ~270 mm
against a 2.4 m head is 2.43 m; and the five dace that got out are stated in the passage
as *a sixth of every dace in the book*, which is why a two-spring tagging total does not
contradict a fifteen-spring count of 31.

## 5. The option-form checklist, written out per question

Method: cover the passage, read only the stem and the four options, and try to beat 1-in-4
on wording. Six routes from the batch20 r1 G-STEM lessons.

**q1 — "What are we told about the counts kept in the book at the trap?"**
1. *Hedge/absolutizer triage*: C is the only hedged option ("may have been") and **C is
   wrong** — the heuristic actively misleads. No option carries an absolutizer.
2. *Contradictory dyad*: none. Variables are start-date (A), a netting comparison (B),
   species identification (C), authorship (D) — four different variables.
3. *Mirrored twin*: none.
4. n/a (not the main-idea item).
5. *Stem announcing shape*: "what are we told about the counts" predicates nothing.
6. *Entailment between options*: none; no option follows from another.
**Residual, stated honestly:** B is the option that most sounds like *a finding* while
A/C/D sound like record-keeping trivia, and a solver who reasons "the tested fact is the
interesting one" gets a nudge. Mitigated (A carries a year plus a causal clause, D makes
a clean general claim) but not eliminated. **Floor: 1-in-4, with a soft lean to B.**

**q2 — "What are we told about the grayling in Kelsingham's study?"**
1. No option is hedged and none carries an absolutizer, so triage returns nothing.
2. Variables: temperature response (A), sample size (B), which attribute matters (C),
   outcome rate (D). A and D are **not** contradictories — both could hold at once.
3. C mirrors a passage claim ("build, not size") but its partner is **not in the set**, so
   the mirror advertises nothing.
5. Neutral referential stem.
6. No entailment.
**Floor: 1-in-4.**

**q3 — "Why, in Kelsingham's account, do so few dace go into the pass?"**
1. No hedges, no absolutizers.
2. Variables: lateral position + attraction flow (A), leaping ability (B), seasonal
   gating (C), shoaling behaviour (D).
3. No mirror.
5. The stem announces *a reason* and **all four options are reasons** — the checklist's
   prescribed fix, applied. A second shape check: A and D are both two-clause, B and C
   single, so "pick the compound one" yields 1-in-2, not the key.
6. No entailment; the stem presupposes only that few dace enter, a fact no other question
   tests.
**Residual:** a solver with real fish-passage knowledge may rank A plausible, because
entrance attraction flow is a standard answer in the field. But B (leaping ability) is the
*equally* standard folk answer, and only the passage's own numbers (23 of 174) separate
them — so domain knowledge does not cleanly pick. **Floor: 1-in-4.**

**q4 — "What is implied about the two orifices cut in 2023?"** — **REBUILT.**
The first draft had A and C as the only two hedged generalisations while B and D were
purpose claims, which let a solver narrow to {A, C} on shape alone — 1-in-2 for free.
Rebuilt so all four are **specific claims about these two openings**, differing only in
content.
1. Only A is hedged ("probably"), and **A is wrong**.
2. Variables: flood magnitude (A), hydraulic purpose (B), position (C), substitution (D).
   A and C are rival explanations but not contradictories on one variable.
3. No mirror.
5. "Implied" would be a tell if only one option went beyond the literal text — but none of
   the four is stated outright in the passage, so the word singles nothing out.
6. No entailment. Key is the shortest option (13 words against 15/15/14).
**Floor: 1-in-4.**

**q5 — "What is this text mainly about?"** — **REBUILT.**
Batch20's main-idea item was killed even after repair because only the key sat at
whole-text altitude. Here **all four options are whole-text noun-phrase summaries** and two
of them (B, C) are *wrong whole-text readings*, not a narrow/broad/off-topic triage set.
Two further fixes came out of the checklist pass:
- C originally read "the hold a disused mill weir keeps on the river above it". That left
  **D as the only option naming more than one species** — and a solver reading the five
  stems alone learns that both dace and grayling are in the piece. C now names both
  species too, so the signal is gone.
- B was given the "X, and Y" shape so the key is not the only two-part summary.
1. No hedges anywhere in the set. 2. No dyad. 3. No mirror. 6. No entailment.
B is the longest option (23 words) and the key is 21.
**Residual:** reading the five stems alone tells a solver there is a pass, a trap with
counts, dace, grayling, and few dace entering. That raises q5 above chance —
**roughly 1-in-3 for q5 on stems alone, 1-in-4 for the other four** — which is inside the
brief's 1-in-2 adversarial floor. It is inherent to a two-species passage: the stems must
name their subjects.

## 12. Law tension, and how it was resolved

RULE 17 wants an ordinary, unremarkable name; law 16 rejects any name with a real bearer;
an ordinary name has ordinary bearers by construction. Resolved exactly as RULE 21
prescribes, and recorded in `generator_meta.rule17_and_rule16_tension_note` so a gate sees
the split rather than inferring it: the ordinary name gets a byline and nothing else, and
the distinctive coinage — under law 16 at full strictness plus the whole RULE 16 distance
burden — carries the speaking and the claim-making.

A second, smaller tension: RULE 17 also complains about coined-name *weave*, while the
lane's habit is four named figures. Resolved by cutting the cast to **two** — one coinage
and one ordinary name — which answers the texture complaint outright and halves the
law-16 exposure at the same time. The cost is that a third name's worth of texture had to
come from elsewhere; it comes from the unnamed hands in the counting book, the mill that
stopped in 1931, and the gravel in the lower orifice.
