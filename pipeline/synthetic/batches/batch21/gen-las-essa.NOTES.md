## 1. Subject and why the skeleton is broken

The binding batch21 bar: five consecutive essä-shorts (b16-003 midsommarstången →
b20-003 handkolorerade vykort) run one skeleton — a **complete documentary corpus** from a
defunct local firm or association, **counted** in the first person, one named **custodian**,
a **limits paragraph**, a **coda quoting one final document**. The instruction was that this
unit must not be an archive-corpus reading at all.

It is not one. There is no collection and no holding institution. **Nothing is counted**: the
only two numbers in the whole passage are `ett par dygn` (how long a coffin stood) and
`tre vintrar` (how long the set stood in the cold room), and neither is a count of a holding.
There is no custodian — the informant is a neighbour, not a keeper. No document is read,
cited or quoted anywhere; the coda quotes nothing. The evidence is remembered custom plus a
few conversations, exactly as the lane brief specified. The two objects that survive into the
present — a sofa suite in Hilding's house, a glass cabinet in a flat's hall — are *not* read
as sources; they are just still there.

The essay's subject is the assigned one and covers every strand the brief listed: what stood
in the room (¶1), when it was opened (¶1, ¶4), who was allowed in (¶2), what the room's
existence said about the household (¶3), why it vanished (¶5–¶6: central heating, the flat,
the television, the visitor who stopped coming, the funeral moving out), and what replaced its
function (the evenings go to the kitchen, the funeral to the chapel and the bygdegård, and the
reception is replaced by nothing because there is nobody left to receive).

## 3. Trap design

**q1 — `enligt_texten_detalj`.** Target: ¶2's stated criterion (`Vem som fick komma in gick
inte efter hur nära man stod varandra, utan efter vad besöket gällde`) plus the three examples
that instantiate it.

- **A (KEY)** paraphrases the criterion: those who came on an errand to the household.
- **B** long-travelled kin — plausible world knowledge with a scope error; closed by
  `oavsett hur långt de hade rest`.
- **C** visits announced in advance — plausible world knowledge the reader *supplies himself*
  (a cold room must be lit in advance, so the visit must have been announced); closed by the
  nurse who was unannounced every other time and shown in anyway.
- **D** the guests the house valued most — **the folk definition of a finrum**, i.e. the answer
  general knowledge gives without reading. Closed twice: those who stood the household closest
  rarely got past the kitchen table, and food (not coffee) was the kitchen's mark.

The design point: on this question **general knowledge points at a distractor**, not at the key.

**q2 — `inference_slutsats`.** The key needs two ¶5 sentences combined: the set was carried out
to the kitchen *because the range was going there anyway*, and the finrum's door then closed
again as before. Nothing says in so many words where the evenings went.

- **A** the set got its place after funerals moved to the chapel — reversed chronology; the
  grandfather's coffin stood in the finrum *years after* the set had been carried out of it.
- **B** the furniture was replaced as the set stood there — an unsupported step; the same sofa
  suite is still in Hilding's house.
- **C (KEY)** the evenings ended up in the kitchen, where the set finally went.
- **D** the set could only stand there once there were radiators — plausible world knowledge as
  a technical precondition; refuted by three winters and a pair of overcoats.

Again the culturally obvious option (D) is a distractor, and the key runs *against* the
received story of the television turning the parlour into the living room.

## 4. RULE 15 — the stems-only analysis, written out

Method: the two prompts and the eight option texts on their own, passage unopened, arguing
actively for every option as a test-wise solver with ordinary Swedish cultural knowledge.

**What a candidate can reach on q1.** `finrummet` is a word most Swedish readers know, and the
folk definition (the room for honoured guests) points at **D**. **A** gets an odd-specificity
lift for being the only option naming a *criterion* rather than a category of person. **C** is
plausible on a practical inference about heating a cold room; **B** is plausible but arbitrary.
Estimate **D 30 / A 28 / C 24 / B 18** — key ≈ 28 %, and the modal blind pick lands on a
distractor.

**What a candidate can reach on q2.** **D** is the technically sensible claim and the strongest
attractor. The key **C** is counter to the received television story, which costs it. A and D
are precondition claims, B and C consequence claims, so the stem's `slutsats` selects no shape.
Estimate **D 32 / C 26 / A 22 / B 20** — key ≈ 26 %.

**Does answering one shift the other? No — in either direction.**
No q2 option mentions visitors, kinship, notice or esteem; no q1 option mentions the
television, heating, furniture or funerals. There is no concessive clause anywhere in either
set, which closes batch20's ONE-WAY LEAK mechanism. Believing q1/A (errand-bearers were let
in) leaves all four q2 options exactly as plausible as before; believing q2/C (the evenings
ended in the kitchen) discriminates none of the q1 options.

**Symmetric vouching.** The two sets are drawn from *different factual sub-worlds of the same
passage* — the reception order of a household (¶2) versus the fate of one object across
¶4–¶5 — and share **zero content tokens** (computed: the only tokens in both sets are the
prepositions `i` and `till`).

**Joint stems-only floor, one number:** ≈ 0.28 × 0.26 ≈ **7 % both-correct against 6.25 %
chance**, a lift of ≈ 1.2×, all of it from the two independent single-question priors and none
from cross-question inheritance.

**Honest residue.** Both questions inevitably sit in one passage world, so a solver who has
guessed the subject can rank options by cultural plausibility — that is what produces the two
priors near 1-in-4. On both questions that heuristic selects a distractor.

## 6. Hedge balance (law 10 / RULE 10 / RULE 13)

- **q1:** no option carries a hedge, a modal or an absolutizer. The key is a flat assertion and
  the "pick the qualified option" heuristic has nothing to select.
- **q2:** the two qualified-sounding options (B `efter hand`, D `kunde`) are **both
  distractors**; the key is a flat claim.

So the hedge heuristic selects the key in **0 of 2** questions — under the rule-10 bar of one
half — and law 10's requirement that on some questions the key be the confident specific claim
is met on **both**. RULE 13: neither question has exactly one qualified option among sweeping
siblings. M-FORM `_ABSOLUTIZERS` hits across both prompts and all eight options: **0**
(including the 2026-08-31 additions `ingenting` / `ingenstans` / `uteslutande`).
