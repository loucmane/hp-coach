# gen-las-debatt — generator notes (batch21, LÄS debatt short)

**Title:** Sjuhundra meter till marknaden
**Family:** `hostmarknad-torg-eller-falt-debatt-short`
**Size / genre:** LÄS short (2 questions), sakprosa / debatt_opinion
**Keys:** q1 **D**, q2 **A**
**Question families:** q1 `enligt_texten_detalj`, q2 `forfattarens_hallning`

The writer is a stallholder who wants the town's autumn market moved from the
old square out to the fairground — and who lost that vote inside her own trade
39 to 12. Everything in the piece is written against her own side.

---

## 1. The reordered five-move skeleton (law 15)

The debatt skeleton is normally: framing-claim → credential → evidence →
announced objection answered → narrowed ask + closing date. Reordered here,
with two of the moves dropped outright:

1. **A lost vote, first sentence.** Not a framing claim, not a decision by an
   authority. `Vid föreningens möte i mars röstade trettionio för att behålla
   höstmarknaden på torget och tolv för att flytta ut den på fältet. Jag var en
   av de tolv…`
2. **The majority's first reason, granted.** The square is *in the way*; people
   walking to the pharmacy or the bus buy what was not on the list. That sale
   does not exist out on the field.
3. **The majority's second reason, granted and left unanswered.** 43 of the 58
   stalls are worked by one person; the pitch is paid for by the day; a second
   day is a fee, not more trade. `Det svaret har jag inte.`
4. **Her own interest disclosed before anyone else can raise it.** She sells
   fruit trees; on the square she carries them ninety metres, on the field the
   car stands behind the stall. *Flyttar marknaden ut tjänar jag på det. Det gör
   inte de flesta andra.*
5. **The placement facts** — 58 pitches, a hard ceiling, the renewal rule, four
   vacancies, seventy-four applicants, and the name pitch 1 is registered in.
6. **The crux.** The market stood on the field until 1969 and was moved in
   because the town wanted it among the shops; a market that only balances
   because it stands in the way of people shopping for something else is an
   appendage to the shops.
7. **The cost conceded, the trial year refused.** The first years will be worse;
   a probationary year teaches nobody anything.
8. **An unnarrowed ask.** *Flytta hela marknaden, båda dagarna, från och med
   nästa höst.*
9. **A concrete-image coda**, then the byline.

**Dropped, per law 15:**
- the **announced objection** — there is no pre-empted critic, no *invändningen
  kommer*-move, and one of the two objections is left standing rather than
  demolished;
- the **"Jag har X i N år" credential** — her only self-positioning is her trade
  and her pitch number in the byline.

**Also dropped:** the glossary (rule 15's "sometimes drop the byline or the
glossary"; the byline is kept, since ~100 % of authentic LÄS passages carry
one). No direct speech anywhere, so no quotation marks at all.

## 3. The arithmetic, and the inference q1 turns on

Stated in the passage: **58** stalls, a hard ceiling (`fler får inte plats`,
with the reason — the fire lane takes four metres across, the chestnuts stand
where they stand); the **renewal rule** (whoever stood there last year stands
there again, `och det vill nästan alla`); **four** pitches fell vacant before
last autumn's market; **74** people sought a pitch for it; **all 58** stalls
were occupied when it opened.

Never stated: **16**.

- 74 − 58 = **16 left without a pitch** — the key.
- Cross-check by the other route: 58 − 4 = 54 renewals; 74 − 54 = 20 new
  applicants; 20 − 4 = **16** refused. Both routes agree.

Residue that deliberately does **not** point at the answer (law 9): 43 of 58
stalls are one-person (that figure belongs to the objection, not to either
question); 130 pitches pegged out on the field; 700 m; 90 m; 1969; the vote
39–12; pitch 1 and pitch 42.

Numeric register is mixed on purpose (rule 15): the counts are spelled out
(*femtioåtta*, *sjuttiofyra*, *hundratrettio*), the year and the two pitch
numbers are digits (*1969*, *Plats 1*, *plats 42*).

## 4. Trap design

**q1 — `enligt_texten_detalj`, stem: _Vilket påstående om platserna på torget
stämmer med texten?_ (key D)**

Every distractor carries an identifiable flaw a careful reader can point to
(law 11 — no verbatim-true distractor):

| opt | variable | trap | why it is wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | allocation method | the rule a reader expects, which the text does not have | the text states a renewal rule in plain words; a map drawn from last year's leaves nothing to the draw |
| B | change in capacity | the passage's own *fyra* re-attached to the wrong quantity | *fyra platser blev lediga* is turnover, not growth; the ceiling is stated as fixed |
| C | order of priority | the renewal rule with its direction inverted — and the set's **only hedged option** (*brukar*) | priority sits with those who already stand there, which is exactly why only four pitches were open to a newcomer |
| **D** | outcome for applicants | **key**, reached by subtraction | 74 sought, 58 were taken; sixteen were left |

**q2 — `forfattarens_hallning`, stem: _Vilken hållning ger textförfattaren
uttryck för?_ (key A)**

| opt | position | why it is wrong |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | move, and carry the worse years | **key** — the text concedes the loss and still asks for the whole move, both days, next autumn |
| B | move, but hold it to one day | uses the objection she admits she cannot answer; not being able to rebut an objection is not yielding to it |
| C | the majority is right, so it should stay | the classic *hållning* trap: her concession (*båda håller*) read as her conclusion. She voted with the twelve |
| D | try the field for a year, then review | the compromise a reader expects — and the one the text refuses in terms (*Ett provår vill jag ändå inte ha*) |

Internal trap taxonomy lives in `generator_meta.planted_traps` only; no
snake_case label, no `KEY X:`, no rule reference and no gate name appears in any
student-visible string. Rationales open `Rätt svar D:` / `Rätt svar A:`.

## 5. RULE 15 — the stems-only analysis, written out

Passage covered. Two stems, eight option texts, read alone.

**What a candidate can actually reach.** From q2's four options a reader learns
that some market might move to a *fält*, that trade could get worse, that a
single day or a trial year are live variants, and that the traders have a
majority view. From q1's four options a reader learns that a square has pitches,
that people apply for them, and that a number of applicants may have gone
without. **Neither set tells the reader anything the other question tests.**

**(a) One-way leak.** Every concessive or presupposing clause was read for what
it asserts as background — the mechanism that killed las-b20-002, where
`även om bidraget fördelades ojämnt` asserted q1's tested proposition and lifted
q1 from ~60 % to ~78 %. Here: q2/A concedes *de sämre åren*; q2/B concedes
nothing and restricts the demand; q2/C asserts the majority is right; q2/D
presupposes only that a move is possible. **None of them says anything about how
pitches are allotted, how many applied, or whether anyone was refused.**
In the other direction, no q1 option states or presupposes any view about where
the market should be held. The two questions were given **disjoint factual
bases** before the options were written, so this holds by construction rather
than by luck. The field's own capacity (130) was deliberately kept out of every
q1 option, because that would have put *fältet* on both sides.

**(b) Mutual entailment / symmetric vouching.**
*Within q1*, the four options sit on four different variables — allocation
method, change in capacity, order of priority, outcome for applicants — so no
two cancel. A and C are two different invented rules that could both be false
(and are); B and D are two different facts that could both be false. There is no
contradictory dyad.
*Within q2*, the four options are four values of **one** variable (move whole /
move halved / stay / stage a trial). That is the deliberate anti-dyad choice: an
earlier draft paired *move, even though trade gets worse* with *stay, even
though loading is awkward* — structural twins on opposite values, exactly the
50 %-for-free shape batch20 flagged. C was rewritten off that mirror, and B and
D were made a **restriction** and a **staging** rather than negations.
*Across the sets*, no symmetric vouching: q1's factual world is the placement
list, q2's is the location decision, and they do not overlap.
Neither question is a mechanism inventory, so the las-b19-003 shape cannot
arise.

**Joint floor, stated as ONE number.** q1 alone ≈ 1-in-3 (D modal, ~30–35 %);
q2 alone ≈ 1-in-3 (A modal, ~35–40 %). **Joint ≈ 0.33 × 0.37 ≈ 12 %
both-correct against 6.25 % chance — a lift under 2×, all of it from the two
single-question priors and none from cross-question inheritance.**

**Honest statement of the residual per-question tells.**
- q1 is at ~1-in-3, not ~1-in-4: **D is the only outcome-shaped option** among
  three arrangement-shaped ones, and a test-wise solver knows a *stämmer med
  texten* item often rewards the concrete outcome. The obvious fix — making a
  second distractor an outcome too — was rejected because the only available
  second outcome (a rival count of refusals) would have formed a contradictory
  dyad with the key, which is worse.
- q2 is at ~1-in-3 with the insändare genre prior favouring the fullest
  position, which is the key. Three of q2's four options involve moving; the
  brief's stance-composition policy (batch16 dom 1) rules that skew within
  tolerance, and the opposing position is present anyway as C.
