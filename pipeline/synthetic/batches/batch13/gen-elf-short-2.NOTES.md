## Why this topic and this cut of it

Assigned topic: why bread goes stale. Law 1 again constrains the cut — a
passage built on the word *retrogradation* would be answerable by anyone who
has read a food-science column. The mechanism is therefore stated once, in
plain words and without terminology ("its starch settles back into an ordered
form"), and the passage's actual subject is an observation from an invented
institute: a *sealed* loaf loses almost no water and still goes stale, while
its crust goes the other way. Two opposite changes, one loaf, nothing lost —
that is the gist, and it is the passage's own, not a recallable fact.

The last two sentences deliberately walk away from the thesis to a consequence
the item never asks about (the trade's crust-squeeze freshness test reads a
loaf backwards), so the passage is not a ramp built toward its own question
(law 9). The science stated is sound: crumb firming without water loss, and
crumb-to-crust moisture migration softening a crust, are both real — the
fictional layer is the institute, the panel, and the observation being reported
as this outfit's finding.

## Self-blind-solve

reuses the passage's own opening clause, and it dies on reading that clause
whole — "do not agree" has the folk explanation as its object, and the scales
join the panels rather than opposing them. **D** is the strongest rival by
world knowledge and has no textual anchor at all. **A** describes a question
the passage never poses. **Single defensible answer; no rewrite needed.**
