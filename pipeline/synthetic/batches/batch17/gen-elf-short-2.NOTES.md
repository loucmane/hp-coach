## The inference

The item is built on the **limit** of a stated rule, not on the rule.

Stated: *"One clause covered the first thirteen weeks: a customer who fell off the
round inside them was struck out of the reckoning, and the price came down by that
customer's weeks of takings."*

Never stated: what happens in week fourteen.

Three facts close the gap:

1. **Nothing held a customer.** "Nothing in the bargain bound a customer", and any
   house could "turn the float away at the gate and take the same milk next
   morning, at the price the dairy fixed, from whoever came by" — so houses could
   leave late as easily as early.
2. **The price was for the round itself.** The pony, float and cans were "valued
   and paid for separately".
3. **The worked case sits inside the window.** Hobbage bought in January, nine
   houses went by April, and £12 12s came back.

One logical inch: the deduction stops at thirteen weeks, departures do not, and
the text offers no other remedy — so a house lost after the window reduced
nothing and its share of the £168 stayed spent. **The buyer alone carried it.**

The stated numbers reconcile exactly — £168 ÷ £21 = eight weeks' takings; 120
houses at £21 a week average 3s 6d each; 9 × 3s 6d × 8 = £12 12s — and the refund
therefore confirms that all nine departures fell inside the window, which is the
case the key asks the reader to look *past*. The item needs only the direction,
so a student who does not convert old money is not blocked.

## Trap architecture

Stem: *What can be concluded here about the sale of a milk round?* The frame is
corpus-attested — "What can be concluded …" occurs in **24 distinct** authentic
ELF stems, including the anchored "here" variants ("What can be concluded here?",
"What can be concluded here in relation to the 17th century silver coins?").
Deliberately **not** the "What is implied here about …" frame used by
`elf-b16-004`, so the two shorts do not read as a pair. It is content-anchored
(law 5) and neutral: all four options are claims about the sale, so no option can
be eliminated as off-topic.

| opt | role | mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| A | **too-literal restatement, one detail bent** | its second clause is straight from the text (the plant *was* valued on its own), which lends the whole option borrowed credibility, and "a hundred and twenty houses" is the most salient figure in the passage. But the price is fixed by takings — "reckoned in weeks of takings" — and the deduction likewise falls "by that customer's weeks of takings", not by a flat per-house sum |
| B | **half-right conjunction** (and the set's only hedged option) | true, quotable premise; "mainly" makes the verdict sound measured. Foreclosed twice: the round was "reckoned in weeks of takings", and "What the houses owed on the day of the change stayed the seller's to collect" — the standing debts were not in the bargain at all |
| **C** | **key** | one_inch_inference off the clause's thirteen-week edge |
| D | **scope-shift** onto the town's milk market | its first clause is a fair reading of the free-switching sentence, so it feels anchored; but the milk came "at the price the dairy fixed" whoever brought it, leaving rival roundsmen nothing to compete on, and the text says nothing about the level of milk prices or any pressure on them |

No distractor is verbatim-true as a whole: A bends the pricing basis, B is true
only in its first conjunct, D imports a consequence the passage never supplies.

## Self-blind-solve

Solved from the passage alone, arguing each non-key option in good faith.

- **A** is the trap for a reader still in retrieval mode, and it is the one that
  most nearly survives, because half of it is quotable. It dies on a single
  phrase the passage repeats in two different sentences: the reckoning is in
  *weeks of takings*, both for the price and for the deduction. Headcount is
  never the unit. (This is why the deduction sentence was rewritten from "by the
  weeks that customer had been counted at" to "by that customer's weeks of
  takings" — the earlier phrasing left the pricing basis derivable but not
  restated, and A was correspondingly harder to defeat.)
- **B** is the strongest rival on feel rather than on evidence: it opens with a
  fact and hedges its verdict, which is exactly the shape a cautious solver
  trusts. It is beaten by one sentence the skimmer passes over — what the houses
  owed stayed with the *seller*, so the debt book was not transferred and cannot
  be what the buyer's money turned on.
- **D** is the outside-knowledge trap: "competition lowers prices" is true in
  general and unsupported here. The dairy fixes the price; the passage reports no
  price level at all.
- **C** is the only option consistent with every rule and figure in the passage,
  and the only one that goes beyond what is written.

**Result: single defensible answer (C). No rewrite of the answer design needed.**

Two passage-level changes were made during the solve. The switching sentence
originally read "at the dairy's price", which admits a reading in which houses
buy *wholesale* at the churn — that would have made D's competition story
arguable. "at the price the dairy fixed" removes the ambiguity and hardens D's
defeat. And the seller-keeps-the-debts sentence was retained (an earlier draft
cut it for length) because without it B has no defeater beyond silence, and the
item goes two-way.

Test-wise checks: no option carries a hard absolutizer (M-FORM clean), and the
sole hedged option is a distractor, so "pick the qualified answer" and "strip the
absolutes" both score nothing. Key letter **C** — not A (addendum rule 1), and
deliberately not B, which is where `elf-b16-004`'s short key sat.
