## 1. Lane, arena, direction — and the bars I had to clear

The lane table binds las-b22-002 to a debatt short that is **not** a rural kommun
withdrawing or charging for a service at a named settlement (b16-002 skolskjuts,
b17-002 variant, b18-002 elljusspår, b19-002 badbrygga, b20-002 plogning — five
units), and **not** a market/torg relocation or a stallholder's first-person voice
(b21-002).

- **Arena:** a regional public theatre (`länsteatern`) and its audience. Not a
  municipality, not a village, no named settlement anywhere in the passage.
- **Direction:** *introducing*. The writer wants surtitles to become the default on
  the main stage from next season. Nothing is withdrawn, nothing is charged for.
- **Money:** there is no kronor figure, no fee, no budget line and no counter-sum in
  the passage. The single sentence that touches money at all — *"Syntolkningens
  pengar vill jag inte heller flytta hit"* — is a refusal to re-divide a budget, and
  it exists to fix the *limit* of her claim, not to carry the argument. The only
  counting in the piece is "four evenings", which is the **object** of the argument
  rather than its engine.
- **Opening:** a printed line in a programme (the category row), not a received
  explanation and not "the payment unit is the wrong unit" (RULE 24, lane bar).
- **Coda:** a concrete image, resolved: *"Skärmen hänger ovanför scenen hela hösten.
  De kvällar då pjäsen spelas på svenska är den mörk."* Not unresolved, not
  aphoristic, not a dated administrative fact; the token `kvar` does not occur under
  any student-facing path (measured: 0).

## 2. Genre and topic rationale

Debattprosa by a single named author with a real position and a real concession.
The topic is passage-specific and not a famous thesis: nothing here is answerable
from world knowledge, because the argument turns on *this* theatre's category
system — surtitles listed as four special evenings between syntolkning and
skolföreställningar, while the same screen texts foreign-language guest performances
as a matter of course.

The concession is genuine and load-bearing rather than decorative: surtitles really
do bind an actor to the script, and the writer says so, signs up to the ensemble's
objection (*"jag skriver under på den"*), and then argues for the change anyway.
That is the piece's thesis shape — **an access provision the institution stages as
an event should become the ordinary default, cost accepted** — and it is distinct
from every other batch22 lane brief I could read.

She also limits her own claim by hand (the small stage is left outside, with a
reason and no proposal), which is the passage's main piece of "concrete residue that
does not point at the answer" and the thing that kills option D.

**Move sequence** (diffed against the lane's five-move skeleton): printed category →
her own hearing and the loop that already serves it → the equipment and the credit
line that already exist → the ensemble's objection, conceded outright → the demand,
scope-limited → what she does not ask for → the dark screen. No announced objection
opener, no "Jag har X i N år" credential, no named local counterpart, no closing
nämnd date, no vote count, no counter-arithmetic. The objection is *conceded*, not
defeated, which inverts the lane's habitual counter-argument move.

## 4. RULE 15, measured as a pair before committing

**(a) One-way leak.** Option by option: every q1 option is about texting policy, the
ensemble's motives or which stage is tried first. None of them predicates anything
about the loop's liveness, coverage, access method or presence in the programme, so
**no proposition about q2's variable is carried by any q1 option**, let alone by two.
In the other direction, all four q2 options presuppose only *that the auditorium has
a hearing loop*; that proposition does survive non-resolution, but it does not
discriminate among q1's options, which are three pro-text stances at different
scopes plus one accusation.

The channel I found and closed: an earlier q2 draft keyed the loop's
**insufficiency** ("it carries the sound, not the words"). The surviving q1
proposition — *the writer wants texting extended* — would then have eliminated the
sufficiency distractor for free, i.e. exactly the strong form this rule bars. The
rebuilt q2 set is **stance-neutral**: a reader who already knows she wants surtitles
everywhere still cannot eliminate "stopped working", "weaker at the back", "missing
from the programme" or "reached through the hearing aid's T setting", because all
four are compatible with that stance. The insufficiency fact stays in the passage as
argumentative texture; it is simply not what q2 turns on.

**(b) Symmetric vouching.** The two sets are drawn from different factual worlds:
q1's wrong options live in the world of the ensemble's motives, the growth rate of
special evenings and the small stage; q2's wrong options live in the world of the
loop's liveness, its behaviour by row and the programme's symbol. No content lemma
is shared between the two option sets, so no member of either set corroborates a
member of the other.

**Joint stems-only solvability (the pair floor, not two per-item floors).** Solved
with the passage covered: q1 ≈ **0.35–0.40** (a debatt writer argues for change,
which eliminates nothing here, because three of four options argue for change; the
concessive-clause shape no longer isolates the key, since B and D carry qualifying
clauses of their own), q2 ≈ **0.25–0.30** (three complaint-shaped options and one
descriptive one — a solver who bets on the descriptive option lands on the key,
which is why I do not claim a clean 0.25). **Joint both-correct floor ≈ 0.10–0.12**,
per-item no better than 1 in 3. Neither item's option set moves the other's
posterior by more than one eliminable option.

Stance-composition: no option asserts the status quo position the writer argues
against. Per the 2026-08-31 owner ruling that is within tolerance, and the two
conditions attached to it hold — the adversarial blind floor is stated above and is
better than 1-in-2, and rule 10 holds (next section).
