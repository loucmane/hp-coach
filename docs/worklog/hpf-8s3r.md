### The three variants

All three meet the design's constraints:
- The badge sits above the passage.
- There is no modal.
- Nothing is placed inside the passage text.
- Reading or answering never needs an extra step.
- Everything stays inside the reading column, so it survives the drill's focus mode (rail collapsed).
- The accent colour and the grading colours are not used.

They differ in badge weight and placement, in how the note behaves after the first display, and in how the caveat attaches.

**A · Marginalen — typographic and quiet.**
- **Badge:** »ÖVNINGSTEXT« is a mono eyebrow line (11 px, weight 600, ink-2) above the passage title. The rail labels beside the question and the outcome get "Övningstext" as a second line; on phone these move above the question and outcome.
- **Note:** an italic colophon at the unit's first display, in the voice of the tactic aside (hairline left rule). On later displays it is absent, and there is no control.
- **Caveat:** a footnote. A `*` marks each LÄS/ELF-based number (11 px minimum), with one footnote per surface.
- **Retired:** the notice stands in italics where the prompt was.
- **For an ADHD-PI reader:** this is the calmest page. There are no new objects or controls and nothing to decide.
- **Cost:**
  - It is the easiest to tune out. On phone the eyebrow lands directly under the linearised "TEXTEN" rail label and reads as more chrome.
  - A note skimmed past once can never be recovered.
  - Footnotes need eye travel. On Framsteg the footnote sits under the ledger, about a screen below the marked hero number.

**B · Etiketten — one object everywhere.**
- **Badge:** a bordered tag with square corners in the house tag style, above the passage title. The same tag:
  - joins the question's rail label once the passage tag has scrolled out of view (its slot is reserved, so nothing shifts);
  - sits above the verdict in feedback;
  - leads a caveat box under every estimate;
  - follows the LÄS/ELF ledger scores;
  - appears dashed on retired history rows.
- **Note:** a box under the tag, open at the unit's first display and folded on later displays. The tag is the toggle (a button with `aria-expanded`), using the house words "läs mer +" / "fäll ihop −". Clicking it is optional.
- **For an ADHD-PI reader:** this is the most recognisable option. The reader learns one object, and it means the same thing in drills, feedback, estimates and history. The note can be re-read whenever the reader wants, but never has to be.
- **Cost:**
  - It is the heaviest variant and adds one tab stop before the options.
  - The estimate surfaces are the busiest of the three (a box per surface plus tags in the ledger).
  - The follow tag fades in (160 ms) as the passage leaves the screen.
  - On phone, tabbing from the top focuses the tag first, which scrolls the reader back to the passage head.

**C · Bandet — a running head.**
- **Badge:** one ruled band per unit, with the badge on the left and the unit title on the right. It sits between the eyebrow and the passage and stays sticky for the whole unit. That keeps it on screen beside the question, the options and the feedback without repeating.
- **Note:** set under the band at the unit's first display, in upright serif. It scrolls away with the passage, and on later displays it is not there. There is no control.
- **Caveat:** one italic line under the estimate, in the same voice as the existing "Linjär skattning — indikativ" disclaimer. There are no markers.
- **History:** a band heads each unit's rows, and the retired unit's band names the notice once.
- **For an ADHD-PI reader:** orientation is constant, since the reader can always see which text the question belongs to. There is one object and no controls, and the caveat uses a voice the reader already knows from Provpass.
- **Cost:**
  - It keeps about 40 px of sticky screen on phone for the whole unit.
  - It meets "beside the question" by staying on screen at the top, not by sitting next to the question.
  - The italic muted line is the easiest of the three caveats to read past.
  - On desktop it has to stack with the live sticky due-header station (see findings).

### Findings for PR4b

1. `DrillQuestion` has no slot for a disclosure, so the bake-off re-composes the chassis from its parts. PR4b needs slots at the passage head, the question rail and the outcome; C also needs a page-level slot.
2. P5 cloze gaps are written `___(N)___` and carry a prompt "Gap (N)". `DrillQuestion` recognises only the authentic format: bare two-digit gaps and an empty ELF prompt. On P5 it would print the raw markers and set "Gap (2)" as the 64 px display headword.
3. P5 exports the passage title in a separate `title` field. `DrillQuestion` derives the heading from a short first paragraph of `context`, so the P5 title would not appear.
4. `PedagogyPanel` appends "." after the option text in "Rätt svar är d) …". P5 options are whole sentences, so this prints "…den..". The bake-off removes the extra full stop.
5. More LÄS/ELF-based estimates than the three in scene 6 need the caveat:
   - DrillResult's `{section}-prognos` on an LÄS/ELF Klart;
   - the section scores on Uppslag (`routes/lektion.tsx`, `formatScore`);
   - arguably the week-delta folio on the NavRail's Framsteg entry.
6. For C only: the desktop drill already has a sticky due-header station in the same column, so a sticky band must stack below it.
7. Pre-existing, outside this lane: `.hpc-m3-stat-d` fails AA in Spalt light (4.28:1). The phone "Nästa →" (`Btn`) has no disabled style, so it looks active before the reader answers.
